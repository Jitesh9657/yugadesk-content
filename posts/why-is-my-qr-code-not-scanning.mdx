QR codes have become one of the easiest ways to share websites, contact information and digital content.

But there is one frustrating problem that many people experience:

**The QR code simply doesn't scan.**

Whether you're printing a QR code on a business card, adding it to product packaging or sharing it online, a code that fails to scan creates a poor user experience.

In this guide, we'll explain the most common reasons QR codes stop working and how you can avoid these mistakes.

---

## 1. The QR Code Is Too Small

One of the biggest mistakes is printing a QR code that is too small. If the camera cannot clearly recognize the pattern, scanning becomes difficult. A QR code should always be large enough to be scanned comfortably from the expected viewing distance.

---

## 2. The Image Quality Is Poor

Low-quality or blurry QR codes are difficult for smartphone cameras to read.

This often happens when:

* screenshots are used
* the image is compressed too much
* the QR code is resized incorrectly

Always download and use the original QR code image.

---

## 3. The Linked URL Is Incorrect

Sometimes the QR code scans successfully, but the destination doesn't work.

Common problems include:

* typing mistakes
* deleted pages
* broken links
* outdated URLs

Before sharing a QR code, test the destination several times.

---

## 4. The QR Code Has Low Contrast

QR codes work best when there is a clear contrast between the foreground and background.

For example:

* black on white ✅
* dark blue on white ✅
* light gray on white ❌
* yellow on white ❌

Poor contrast makes scanning more difficult.

---

## 5. The QR Code Is Damaged

Printed QR codes may become:

* scratched
* folded
* faded
* partially covered

If important parts of the code are missing, scanning reliability decreases.

---

## 6. The Camera Is Too Far Away

Distance matters. Holding the phone too far away or too close can prevent successful scanning. Move the camera slowly until the QR code comes into focus.

---

## 7. The QR Code Is Curved

QR codes printed on bottles, cups or curved packaging can become distorted. Distortion changes the alignment of the QR pattern, making it harder for cameras to recognize. Whenever possible, place QR codes on flat surfaces.

---

## 8. The Destination Website Loads Slowly

Sometimes the QR code scans correctly, but users think it failed because the website takes too long to load. Optimizing your website for mobile devices helps create a better experience after the scan.

---

## 9. The QR Code Was Never Tested

Many people generate a QR code and immediately print thousands of copies.

Instead, test it on:

* Android phones
* iPhones
* different camera apps
* different lighting conditions

A few minutes of testing can prevent expensive mistakes later.

---

## 10. The QR Code Was Edited After Creation

Cropping, stretching or adding excessive design elements can damage the QR code.

Avoid:

* stretching the image
* removing margins
* covering the center with large logos
* applying heavy filters

Keep the QR code as close to the original as possible.

---

## Best Practices for Better QR Codes

To improve scan success:

* use high-resolution images
* maintain good contrast
* test before printing
* keep adequate size
* verify the destination URL
* avoid unnecessary editing

Following these practices makes QR codes more reliable across different devices.

## Why Businesses Should Care

Every failed scan represents a missed opportunity.

Customers may never reach your:

* website
* online store
* booking page
* contact information
* promotional campaign

A reliable QR code improves customer experience and increases engagement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does my QR code scan on one phone but not another?

Different smartphones use different cameras and scanning software. Testing on multiple devices helps identify compatibility issues.

### Can resizing a QR code stop it from working?

Improper resizing can reduce image clarity and make scanning difficult.

### Does internet speed affect QR codes?

The QR code itself scans without internet, but opening the linked website requires a connection.

### Should I test my QR code before printing?

Yes. Testing on several devices is one of the easiest ways to avoid publishing a broken QR code.
